Instructions

Theory

A Neubauer haemocytometer is a counting chamber with an etched grid. Each large square is 1 mm × 1 mm and the chamber is 0.1 mm deep, so one large square holds 0.1 µL (10−4 mL). The mean count per large square multiplied by 104 therefore gives cells per mL of what was loaded; multiplying by the dilution factor gives the concentration of the original suspension.

Dye exclusion: live cells have intact membranes that exclude dyes such as trypan blue, so they look bright and clear. Dead cells take up the dye and look blue.

cells/mL = mean count per square × dilution factor × 104
viability (%) = live ÷ (live + dead) × 100

Which cells to count

  • Count the cells inside each large square you use — typically the four corner squares, and the centre square if you want a fifth.
  • For cells on a boundary, count those touching the top and left lines (marked green on the grid) and ignore those touching the bottom and right lines, so no cell is counted twice.
  • Record unstained cells as live and blue-stained cells as dead.
  • Count a clump as its individual cells where you can tell them apart; if the sample is very clumpy, resuspend and reload.
  • As a guide, aim for roughly 20–100 cells per large square. Dilute further if it is too crowded, or resuspend in a smaller volume if there are too few.

Preparing the cell suspension

  1. Centrifuge the cells to form a pellet.
  2. Aspirate the media without disturbing the pellet.
  3. Resuspend the pellet in a known volume of media and mix to a single-cell suspension.
  4. Take a 10 µL aliquot of the cell suspension.
  5. Add it 1:1 to 10 µL of exclusion dye (e.g. 0.4% trypan blue) — a dilution factor of 2.
  6. Mix well by gentle pipetting.
  7. Transfer 10 µL into the haemocytometer chamber under the coverslip.
  8. View with a microscope and count promptly, as prolonged exposure to the dye is toxic to cells.

Using this app

  • Type counts into the squares, or select a square and use the tally clicker. Press Lock page to stop the page scrolling while you tally.
  • If the cells were counted elsewhere (e.g. an automated counter), type the cells/mL straight into the Results boxes.
  • Enter the resuspension volume to get the total number of cells.
  • Change the dilution factor if you did not mix 1:1 with dye.
  • Open Plate seeding to work out the media and cell suspension volumes for your wells, dishes or flasks.
